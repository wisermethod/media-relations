# Broadcast pitches

This medium file adds broadcast facts and choices when Pitch loads it.

## What is known about the show?

Read the reporter file's base Identity, Role, Record and Routes, and its journalist Outlets, Audience, Formats, Sources quoted, Style and Preferences. Gather the show's name, its host, the segment addressed, its audience, the story types and formats it runs, the addressee's route to booking, the contact route and the booking or production timing. A format line saying the desk takes segments does not establish the segment's style. Use the gathering hand-off in Pitch's medium step for show facts the file lacks.

Use those facts to state why this story and offer fit this show and segment. Name each fact still missing in the package.

## What can the desk film or hear?

Read the requester's material for a person affected by the story or a guest who can speak to it. Gather what the desk can film or hear: action, supplied footage or b-roll, archival visuals, a location, or an interview guest's presence and what they will show or explain. Gather access and availability for the people, assets and location, each with its source.

What does this format need? Take the first match.

- The file or show evidence establishes a television or video interview show. The guest's presence and what they will show or explain is the asset. No separate b-roll is required.
- The file or show evidence establishes a television or video news or feature segment that is not a guest interview. It needs a person affected and something to film or hear.
- The file or show evidence establishes a radio show or segment. It needs a speaker, segment fit and something to hear. The evidence on radio-specific booking is thin; carry that limitation in the package.
- The format's need is not established. Name that gap. Ask what this format needs. Stop until established; claim no format requirement the evidence does not hold.

Are the person and asset this format needs in hand? Take the first match.

- The person, including an interview guest, or the asset, including what that guest will show or explain, is missing. Name each gap. Ask for the missing material or a narrower offer this format can use. Stop until supplied or narrowed.
- Both are in hand. Record them and their sources.

Guest readiness is gathered in Pitch's medium step.

## Which choices apply?

Put each applicable choice below to the requester at Pitch's contested-choices step, with its evidence and recommendation. Record the answer there.

- **Lead time.** Applicable to a first pitch to a broadcast show or segment when `podcast.md` is not loaded. Muck Rack's Guide to Media Pitching describes lead times from weeks to the same day. Put the show's stated booking window beside the requester's proposed timetable. Recommend the sourced window where one is known. Without one, name it as missing and ask which timetable the requester wants.
- **After the show.** Applicable only to a live or scheduled show whose airing time is known. Michael Smart's show-checking guidance recommends approaching just after it airs. Put that option beside the show's stated preference and Pitch's daytime choice. Recommend the stated preference where known; otherwise recommend after-air contact. A chosen send past the pitch date requires re-reading the file and answering Is a flag in force? and Are the currency fields current? as of the new pitch date before drafting.
- **Supplied footage.** Applicable to a television or video desk where supplied footage is in hand. Offer it now or keep it for a request. Michael Smart's four-ways account says top network producers may reject staged supplied footage where local affiliates may accept it. Recommend the target's stated preference where known; otherwise recommend offering the footage with what it depicts stated, leaving acceptance to the producer.
- **News-segment angle.** Applicable to a television or video news or feature segment that is not a guest interview. Michael Smart's four-ways account describes an angle written like the producer's own short internal pitch. Put that framing beside a direct statement of the story. Recommend the short internal pitch unless a stated desk preference points elsewhere. Record the selected angle.

## What is added to the package?

Add the show and segment name, host, audience, formats, route to booking, contact and timing; the show and segment fit with its evidence; the person or guest and what they will show or explain; the readiness facts gathered by Pitch; the assets, access and availability; each missing item and any explicit go-on for missing readiness; the radio limitation where it applies; and the applicable choices with their evidence and the requester's answer. Keep each fact's source, date, hedge and evidence label.
