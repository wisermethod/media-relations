# Broadcast pitches

Load when Pitch's medium step names a television, radio, streaming-video or video interview show, a segment, or its producer or booker. This file adds gathering and package facts to Pitch's workflow. It writes no prose. Every message stays with `skills/Content Author/` in `wiser`.

## What is known about the show?

Read the reporter file's base Identity, Role, Record and Routes, and its journalist Outlets, Audience, Formats, Sources quoted, Style and Preferences. Gather the show's name, its host, the segment addressed, its audience, the story types and formats it runs, the contact route and the booking or production timing. Role and published segments may name the show and host; do not assume the target producer is the host. A format line saying the desk takes segments does not establish the segment's style.

Which items are missing? Take the first match.

- One or more. Name each to the requester. Run `skills/External Research/` in `wiser` by name for the missing show facts. Hand `<research_request>` as a natural-language question about those items, naming this pitch package as the consumer. Hand `<scope>` with this show, its professional public record, the pitch date and Standard depth. Hand `<source_material>` with the relevant file lines and any supplied show pages. Use the returned brief with its sources, dates, hedges and evidence labels. A fact still absent is named as missing before drafting and in the package.
- None. Use the file's facts with their sources and dates.

Michael Smart's national-TV guidance and his show-checking guidance ask the pitcher to know the host, segment, audience, story types, format, contact and timing. Use those facts to state why this story and offer fit this show and segment. Do not fill an absent fact from the show's name or reputation.

## What can the desk film or hear?

Read the requester's material for a person affected by the story or a guest who can speak to it. Gather what the desk can film or hear: action, supplied footage or b-roll, archival visuals, a location, or an interview guest's presence and what they will show or explain. Gather access and availability for the people, assets and location, each with its source. Michael Smart's four ways to place more TV stories and the Unity Park case in Built not Born show how people, filmable action, footage and prepared locations make production possible.

What does this format need? Take the first match.

- A segment needs a person or a picture or sound the material does not hold. Name each gap in the package facts and put it to the requester before drafting. Ask for the missing person or asset, or a narrower offer the format can use. Stop until they supply it or choose that offer. Do not invent either.
- An interview show. The guest's own presence may be the asset. Record what they can show or explain and why it fits this format. Do not require separate b-roll where the show does not need it. A guest or that account is missing: put the gap to the requester and stop until it is supplied or the offer is narrowed.
- The format's person and asset are in hand. Record them and their sources.
- The file and show evidence do not establish what the format needs. Name that gap. Ask which offer the requester wants to make. Stop until they answer; claim no format requirement the evidence does not hold.

Where supplied footage is offered, put its suitability to the requester. Smart's four-ways account says top network producers may reject staged supplied footage where local affiliates may accept it. The target's stated preference governs where it is known. Otherwise recommend offering the footage with what it depicts stated, leaving acceptance to the producer. Record the choice. This consideration is not a ban.

## What shows the guest is ready?

Gather a past appearance or video showing how the spokesperson interviews, their name pronunciation, and their availability. Record each as a package fact with its source, or name it as missing. Do not call the guest good on camera or on air without evidence. Cision's Newsjacking Dos and Donts recommends a video showing interview ability and a pronunciation note.

## How does the angle reach Content Author?

Hand Content Author an instruction to make the email's angle read like the producer's own short internal pitch or an on-air tease, using the story the sources hold. Michael Smart's four-ways account and his account of a CNN producer's reaction to pitches support that framing. This is direction for the email, not a request to write a recorded script. Content Author's loaded row still governs the email and subject lines.

## Which timing and channel choices apply?

Put each applicable choice below to the requester at Pitch's contested-choices step, with the evidence and a recommendation. Keep choices already answered. These choices concern the package they decided to pursue; they do not decide whether now is the time to pitch at all.

- **Lead time.** Muck Rack's Guide to Media Pitching describes lead times from weeks to the same day. A planned feature or guest booking and a daily news segment may need different preparation. Recommend the show's stated booking window where one is sourced. Without one, ask which timetable the requester wants and name the booking window as missing. Do not impose one interval.
- **After the show.** Smart's show-checking guidance recommends approaching just after the show airs. Put that option beside the show's stated preference and the daytime choice Pitch already presents. Recommend the stated preference where known; otherwise offer after-air contact where the airing time is known. No known airing time: name it as missing and retain Pitch's daytime default as a choice.
- **A call after email.** Smart describes this as common for local assignment desks; his account of calls after email reports requests for information and coverage from one practitioner's experience. Put a call after the email beside email alone, with that limit on the evidence and the email preference Pitch already presents. Recommend email first, then a call where the requester needs time-sensitive coordination and the desk's preferences allow it. No call is made by this skill.

## What carries over to radio?

A speaker, fit to the segment, something to hear, access and availability carry over. The evidence in hand is thin on radio: it does not supply radio-specific audio, segment-length or booking guidance. Say so in the package. Do not turn television's visual requirements into a radio rule.

## What is added to the package?

Add the show and segment fit with its evidence; the person or guest and what they can show or explain; readiness facts; the assets, access and availability; each missing item; and the applicable choices with their evidence and the requester's answer. These are additions to Pitch's one package, not another package or workflow. The Content Author request carries the same facts and gaps, subject to Pitch's held-detail rules. Missing readiness evidence stays a named gap, never a claim of readiness.
