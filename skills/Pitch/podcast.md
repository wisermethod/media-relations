# Podcast pitches

Load when Pitch's medium step names a podcast, its host or its booker. This file adds gathering and package facts to Pitch's workflow. Every message stays with `skills/Content Author/` in `wiser`. It never writes an episode, questions for the host or a recorded script. A recorded script stays the gap declared on Content Author.

## What is known about the show?

Read the reporter file's base Identity, Role, Record and Routes, and its journalist Outlets, Audience, Formats, Sources quoted, Style, Preferences and Reach. Gather the show's name, host, listeners, format, recent episodes and past guests. Use episodes and guests the record actually states, not names in headline lists. A format line saying the desk takes episodes does not establish the show's style.

Which items are missing? Take the first match.

- One or more. Name each to the requester. Run `skills/External Research/` in `wiser` by name for the missing show facts. Hand `<research_request>` as a natural-language question about those items, naming this pitch package as the consumer. Hand `<scope>` with this show, its professional public record, the pitch date and Standard depth. Hand `<source_material>` with the relevant file lines and any supplied show pages. Use the returned brief with its sources, dates, hedges and evidence labels. A fact still absent is named as missing before drafting and in the package.
- None. Use the file's facts with their sources and dates.

## Why do these listeners fit?

State why the show's listeners match the audience the story is for, from the file or the show evidence. Michael Smart's Pitching podcasts recommends audience fit even for a small show. His Solving the podcast problem distinguishes downloads from listens and calls for listening and demographic evidence. Preserve any reach figure's metric and period. Do not turn downloads into listens or treat a larger show as better by size. A filmed podcast keeps this audience-fit rule when `broadcast.md` also loads.

Can the evidence support audience fit? Take the first match.

- It can. Carry that fit and its sources into Pitch's fit statement.
- It cannot. Name what is missing. Ask for audience evidence. Stop until it is supplied; a reach figure alone does not fill the gap.

## What is the episode offer?

Gather an episode idea that fits this show's style, the guest and what they bring, and a reference to a past episode or guest the host will recognise. The idea fits the decided story; do not find a different story here. Source the guest's contribution from the requester's material and the show connection from the record or show evidence. Smart's account of James Clear's launch tour describes individual pitches referencing past guests; his account of non-journalist podcasters calls for an idea in the show's style.

Is each part supported? Take the first match.

- The episode idea, the guest's contribution or the past reference lacks support. Name each gap. Ask for the material, or a narrower offer or reference the sources hold. Stop until supplied or narrowed. Do not invent a guest, an episode or a connection.
- All three are supported. Carry the offer and its sources into the angle and Content Author request. The episode idea describes what a conversation could explore; it is not the conversation or a list of host questions.

## Which approach fits this host?

Take the first match. Put the choice to the requester at Pitch's contested-choices step before drafting. Smart's What non-journalist podcasters think says a non-journalist host may want to develop an idea together, while a journalist wants the strongest material first. Present that distinction as evidence for a choice, not a rule for every host.

- The file or show evidence establishes a non-journalist host. Offer developing the idea together or presenting the strongest material first. Recommend the collaborative approach unless a stated preference points elsewhere.
- The file or show evidence establishes a journalist host. Offer the same choice. Recommend the strongest material first unless a stated preference points elsewhere.
- Neither is established. Name the host's kind as unknown. Ask which approach the requester wants. Do not infer it from the podcast format or the reporter file's name.

Record the evidence, recommendation and answer. This choice shapes the episode offer and ask; it does not replace Pitch's opening choice or Content Author's row.

## Is the appearance tied to a launch?

Take the first match.

- It is. Put months of booking lead time to the requester as a planning consideration. Smart's account of James Clear's launch tour says pitches went out four months before publication and requested a launch-week release. That is one case, not every show's booking window. Gather the launch date, the desired recording and release timing, and any booking window the show states. Recommend working back from that window; where none is known, ask which timetable the requester wants. Name what is missing. A request for launch-week release is not an agreement or a promise. Record their choice before drafting.
- It is not. Use the show's stated timing where known and Pitch's applicable timing choices. Do not impose the launch-tour interval.

## What is added to the package?

Add the listener and show fit with its evidence; the host and format; the recent episodes and past guests used; the episode idea, guest and their contribution; the past reference; any supplied guest-readiness facts and assets; each missing item; and the host-approach and timing choices with their evidence and the requester's answer. These are additions to Pitch's one package, not another package or workflow. The Content Author request carries the same facts and gaps, subject to Pitch's held-detail rules. It requests the pitch email only.
